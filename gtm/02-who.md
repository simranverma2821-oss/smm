# Humble Solutions GTM — The "Who" (ICP)

The second W, and the most important for outbound. **Who** will buy from HS?

This is the Ideal Customer Profile (ICP). A weak ICP says "startups and SMBs that need software." That is useless for targeting. A 10x ICP is ranked, specific enough to become a search filter, and just as clear about who we refuse as who we chase.

Decision up front: **we lead with one beachhead segment, not three at once.** Spreading across every buyer type is why word of mouth never turns into a repeatable pipeline.

---

## The CARE Prompt (customized for HS)

CARE stands for **C**ontext, **A**ction, **R**esult, **E**xample.

### Context

You are a good GTM (Go-to-market) researcher. You are doing research for a company called Humble Solutions and its website is https://humblesolutions.in.

### Action

Define the ICP (Ideal Customer Profile) for this company.

### Result

This ICP should outline the common characteristics and attributes of the company's most promising potential clients. Include:

1. Countries these ICP companies operate in
2. Primary industries
3. Employee size (ranges)
4. Annual revenue range
5. Specific technologies or tools they use
6. Company or industry level keywords
7. Relevant buying signals or intent
8. Any other prioritization signal

### Example (format)

Give a table with two columns: ICP Criteria and Values. List multiple values where possible. Highlight essential ICP features. Use bullet points for clarity. Conclude with a short summary.

## The decision: three tiers, one beachhead

| Tier | Segment | Why | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1 (BEACHHEAD)** | Western digital agencies and software studios needing white-label dev and AI capacity | Already have clients and budget, buy repeatedly (MRR), technical so they trust our stack, short sales cycle, pay in USD/GBP/EUR | 70% |
| Tier 2 | Funded early-stage startups (seed to Series A) in the West | Need to ship fast, will take dedicated devs and AI builds, high urgency | 20% |
| Tier 3 | SMBs with existing software wanting AI added | Large market, but must be educated first, longer to close | 10% |

Government projects are a separate track, not an ICP. They are won through procurement portals (GeM, tenders), not outbound. Kept out of this file on purpose.

**Why Tier 1 is the beachhead:** one agency is not one sale. It is a partner who sends work for years. They do not need to be sold on "why software matters," they need overflow capacity and AI skills they cannot hire fast enough. That is the lowest-friction path from freelance chaos to real MRR.

## Buying signals (the 10x layer — this decides WHEN to reach out)

Firmographics tell you who fits. Buying signals tell you who is ready now. Prioritize any target showing these, ranked strongest first.

**For agencies (Tier 1):**
1. Open job posts for React, Node, iOS, Android, or AI or ML engineers. They have demand they cannot staff. This is the single best signal.
2. Recently posted a case study or service page about AI. They are selling AI but may not have the team to deliver it.
3. Hiring on Upwork, Contra, or LinkedIn for contract developers.
4. Agency owner posts about being slammed, growing, or turning work away.
5. Recent large client win announced publicly.

**For startups (Tier 2):**
1. Funding announced in the last 6 to 12 months (Crunchbase, LinkedIn).
2. Actively hiring engineers.
3. Founder publicly looking for a dev team or technical help.

**For SMBs (Tier 3):**
1. Public posts or job ads about automation, efficiency, or AI adoption.
2. Growing headcount in operations to handle manual volume.

## Negative ICP (who we refuse — saying no is the 10x move)

Chasing these drains time and drags margins down. Deprioritize or decline.

- Individuals wanting a full app for 200 to 500 USD. Wrong budget, endless scope.
- Clients demanding equity instead of cash for build work.
- Buyers who want a fixed price for an undefined scope and will not do discovery.
- One-off projects with zero recurring or referral potential, when pipeline is full.
- Markets with high payment risk or where budgets cannot support USD-level rates.
- Direct competitor dev shops looking to resell at no margin, unless it is a real white-label deal.

Every hour spent on a negative-ICP lead is an hour not spent landing a Tier 1 agency.

- Headcount: 11 to 50
- Title: Founder, CEO, Managing Director, CTO, Head of Delivery
- Keywords in company or bio: "agency", "studio", "web development", "app development", "digital product", "AI"
- Layer a buying signal: cross-check against companies with open developer job posts (LinkedIn Jobs, their careers page)

That combination is a ready-to-work outbound list, not a vague audience.

## Summary

HS sells to three tiers, but we lead with one: **Western digital agencies and software studios (5 to 50 people) that need white-label mobile, web, and AI capacity they cannot hire fast enough.** They already have budget and clients, they buy repeatedly, and the relationship compounds into MRR. Tier 2 funded startups and Tier 3 SMB AI adopters are secondary. Government is a separate procurement track. The strongest signal to act on is a target actively hiring developers, and the discipline to refuse negative-ICP leads is what keeps the focus sharp.
